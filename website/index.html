01 — How it works

Point it at your photos. It does the sorting.

  1. 01

    Choose a folder or a drive

    Your photos stay exactly where they are. Nothing is copied, moved or renamed, even at 200,000 photos.

  2. 02

    Smriti sorts them on your computer

    Faces grouped into people, places named, dates fixed, duplicates and bursts found. No upload, no account.

  3. 03

    Find and relive anything

    Search, browse a person or a trip, and find new memories and collages each time you open it, even on a plane.

Most photo apps want to be a service. Smriti is a tool: nothing runs when it's closed, there's no subscription, and the whole library travels with the drive.

11 — network honesty

Online only when
you ask.

Your photos never leave your machine. No telemetry, no analytics, no "anonymous usage statistics" — in the app or on this website. Here's everything Smriti ever fetches; the privacy notes list every request in detail.

  1. 01

    Map tiles

    When you open the Map view. Tiles are cached on your drive after first load; subsequent panning is served from cache.

    tile.openstreetmap.org
    Disable: avoid the Map view
  2. 02

    Smart features — one time, opt-in

    The models for faces and visual search, plus offline place names. Downloaded once, checked against published checksums, then everything runs on your machine. Skip it and the rest of the app still works.

    github.com · huggingface.co
    Disable: choose Later when offered
  3. 03

    Update check — opt-in

    If you opt in, Smriti checks for new releases once every 24 hours. Off by default. No photo data, no telemetry, just a single HEAD-style request.

    api.github.com/repos/.../releases/latest
    Default: off
  4. 04

    Update install — only when you click Download

    If you accept an update, Smriti fetches the matching installer for your platform and verifies its SHA-256 against the release's SHA256SUMS file. No metadata about your install or library is sent.

    github.com/.../releases/download/...
    Disable: don't click Download
12 — design discipline

Offline by default.
Never by dogma.

Face recognition is the one slow step. On CPU, a 90,000-photo library takes ~3 hours. If you want to skip the wait, Smriti can borrow a free Kaggle or Colab GPU for the one-time pass — and finish in minutes.

It sends only 112×112 aligned face crops. Never originals, never metadata, never filenames. When the pass is done, you disconnect. Forever, not "until the next sync."
